Getting your flights in

How do I import my logbook from another app?

Tap the import button at the top of the Logbook, or go to Settings › Data › Import a logbook. Choose a PDF logbook in the EASA format, or a table exported from another app: CSV, TSV, text or Excel (.xlsx). The import assistant recognises the columns; check them, review the flights, then import. Numbers files and old .xls workbooks must first be exported as CSV or .xlsx. An import can be undone in Settings › Data › Imports.

Can the app read my paper logbook?

Yes. In Logbook › … › Read the paper logbook, photograph your pages or choose photos you have already taken. The pages are read on your device, and you check every line before the flights are added.

How do I import my airline roster?

Open Logbook › … › Import the roster, or Settings › Data › Import the roster. Choose the calendars your roster syncs to on the iPhone, or an .ics file exported from your airline’s crew app. Flights you have already flown are added with their planned times and marked “To complete”: enter the actual times and save each flight. Nothing in your calendar is changed.

Exporting your flights

How do I export my logbook?

In Logbook › … › Export, choose PDF logbook (EASA) for a print-ready logbook in the EASA format, A4 landscape, or Spreadsheet (CSV) for all your flights in a table. Then save or share the file wherever you like.

Subscription

What happens after 50 flights?

Flare is free for your first 50 flights, with every import, the export, the widget and the guides. Only flights count: simulator sessions and positioning do not. Beyond that, adding flights requires Flare Pro (€29.99 per year, or €79.99 once for life): saving a flight or an import opens the subscription screen, and an import that would go past the limit is held back whole, with what you checked still on screen, until you subscribe. Without the subscription, or once it has ended, your logbook stays readable, editable and exportable (EASA PDF, CSV).

How do I restore my purchase?

In the app, go to Settings › Subscription and tap Restore purchases; the subscription screen has the same button. Your device must use the Apple Account you subscribed with: the subscription belongs to that account, and there is no other account to sign in to.

How do I manage or cancel my subscription?

In the app, go to Settings › Subscription › Manage subscription, or open the Settings app on your iPhone, tap your name, then Subscriptions. Subscriptions are handled by Apple: Flare Pro renews every year unless you cancel it at least 24 hours before the end of the current period, and after cancelling it stays active until the end of the period already paid for. For a refund, use Apple’s Report a Problem page.

Your data

Where is my logbook stored? Does it sync with iCloud?

Your logbook is stored on your device. With Settings › Data › Sync with iCloud, it is also kept in your own private iCloud database and shared between your iPhone and iPad signed in to the same Apple Account; the developer has no access to it. When sync is off, your logbook stays on the device only.

How do I delete my data?

In the app, go to Settings › Data › Erase the whole logbook. Deleting the app also deletes its data from your device. Data stored in iCloud can be deleted in your iPhone’s Settings › [your name] › iCloud, in the storage management. More in the privacy policy.

Does the app need an internet connection?

No. Everything works offline. Only the flight photos (cities, aircraft, satellite views) and the tiles of the flight map need the network; offline, the app shows those it has already kept.

Languages and devices

Which languages does the app support?

English, French, German, Spanish and Italian. The app follows your device’s language; to choose another one, go to Settings › Language in the app, or to the app’s page in your iPhone’s Settings.

Does it work on iPad?

Yes: the same app runs on iPhone and iPad.

Is Flare approved by EASA?

No. Flare follows the logbook format of AMC1 FCL.050 and computes the rules that go with it (FCL.060 recency, ORO.FTL.210 limits), but it is neither affiliated with nor approved by EASA. As the pilot, you remain responsible for the accuracy of your logbook.
